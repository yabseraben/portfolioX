01. The Context

Coming Into the Summer

When I joined Snowflake's Analytics Marketing team in June 2024, the team was sitting on a goldmine of insights. Through our marketing working group, we'd identified that mid and bottom-of-funnel content about migrations—especially virtual hands-on labs—was driving incredibly strong engagement. But we faced two key challenges: how to extend campaign reach by supplementing our promotion with partner-driven budgets, and how to create more targeted, source-specific messaging that would resonate with customers considering migrations from legacy systems like SQL Server.

KEY INSIGHT
Mid/bottom-of-funnel migration content + virtual hands-on labs were driving strong engagement, but we needed to amplify reach through partner budgets and create more source-specific messaging.

At the same time, Snowflake Summit had just concluded, leaving us with an incredible library of content: customer sessions focused on migration, "What's New" product launches, a SnowConvert AI demo, and partner-submitted content. The challenge? All of this high-impact material was sitting in different places without a clear way to package it for mid-funnel engagement. We needed a vehicle to connect it, amplify it, and keep the momentum going post-Summit.

This context set the stage for my three major projects: AI Data Cloud Day, SQL Server Migration Messaging, and the Customer Migration eBook. Each project was designed to address a specific gap—whether it was content amplification, competitive positioning, or sales enablement—while supporting Snowflake's broader goal of positioning the AI Data Cloud as the modernization platform for the enterprise.

Project 01: AI Data Cloud Day

AI Data Cloud Day became the answer to our post-Summit challenge. This Microsoft-partnered virtual event, hosted on August 6th, was designed to provide enterprise data architects and technical leaders with practical strategies for migrating off legacy systems and modernizing their data for the AI era.

The Strategic Opportunity: By partnering with Microsoft and leveraging MDF (Marketing Development Funds), we could extend our promotional reach beyond Snowflake's owned channels. The timing—right after Summit—allowed us to repackage strong Summit content into a focused, partner-led campaign that would keep the modernization conversation alive.

Project 02: SQL Server Migration Messaging

While AI Data Cloud Day focused on broad modernization themes, we recognized a more targeted opportunity: SQL Server migrations. Snowflake has historically been successful with these migrations, but we lacked source-specific messaging and positioning that sales teams could use in competitive situations.

The Goal: Build a scalable messaging framework that moves customers from legacy pain points (SQL Server) to AI-readiness (Snowflake), with supporting assets for sales enablement and web presence.

THE RESEARCH PROCESS
I shadowed Cindy in customer conversations, partnered with the Competitive Intelligence team for insights, interviewed acquisition AEs actively running SQL Server migrations, and reviewed third-party research plus existing customer stories. This multi-angle approach ensured our messaging was grounded in real customer pain points and competitive dynamics.
Week 1-2 • Research Phase
Understanding the Customer Reality
Through conversations with competitive intelligence and acquisition AEs, three pain points emerged consistently: (1) Rising costs and rigid legacy architecture with SQL Server, (2) Data silos slowing down collaboration, and (3) Fragmented governance creating security risks. These became the foundation for our messaging pillars.
Week 3-4 • Messaging Development
Building the Three-Pillar Framework
We developed messaging under three pillars that directly addressed SQL Server limitations: (1) Easily Modernize on a Proven AI Data Cloud with Instant Scalability—leverage your SQL skillset on Snowflake's AI-native platform with automated admin and instant elasticity. (2) Break Down Internal & External Silos for Seamless Connectivity—enable zero-copy sharing, open table format interoperability, and access to third-party data via Snowflake Marketplace. (3) Unify Security & Governance—simplify compliance with automatic sensitive data detection, centralized governance, and cross-cloud business continuity.
Week 5-6 • Asset Development
From Framework to Execution
With messaging finalized, we applied it to refresh the SQL Server to Snowflake webpage—updating copy to align with the three pillars, reordering benefit sections for narrative clarity, and submitting the Wrike ticket for coordination with web, brand, and editorial teams. In parallel, I began building a customer story pipeline by interviewing AEs and identifying SQL Server wins that could feed future enablement content.

The Impact: The messaging framework is now being used across sales enablement materials, the refreshed webpage, and internal reference documents. More importantly, it established a repeatable model: what worked for SQL Server can be applied to other legacy sources like Teradata and Oracle.

KEY LEARNING
Partnering with the Competitive Intelligence team was transformational. Their perspective helped sharpen positioning in ways that directly addressed competitor narratives. Great messaging isn't just about what you say—it's about understanding the entire competitive landscape.

Project 03: Customer Migration eBook

Snowflake has a strong portfolio of customer migration stories—spanning industries, use cases, and migration types. The gap? These stories lived in different places and formats. For prospects, partners, or sales teams, there was no single, easy-to-access asset that pulled them together into a unified narrative.

The Opportunity: Create a consolidated, gated eBook that would serve multiple functions: inspire technical and business decision-makers with real-world examples, accelerate pipeline by giving sales teams credible proof points, and integrate seamlessly into H2 campaigns tied to modernization and AI transformation.

CONTENT SELECTION FRAMEWORK
I created a structured framework to ensure we had both variety and relevance: (1) Industry & brand recognition—prioritize well-known companies across diverse sectors. (2) Strong metrics & outcomes—include quantifiable business and technical results. (3) Migration type variety—showcase SQL Server, Teradata, PostgreSQL, and cloud-native migrations. (4) Alignment with AI modernization—ensure stories supported our broader transformation narrative.

Using this framework, I reviewed hundreds of case studies, Summit sessions, and partner-submitted content to shortlist 12 compelling stories. Featured customers included PayPal (PostgreSQL migration with 500M payments processed in 3 months), Marriott (legacy system modernization), and Pfizer (data warehouse transformation)—spanning retail, hospitality, healthcare, and financial services.

I coordinated with customer marketing, partner marketing, and editorial teams to get legal approval and story polish. The challenge was balancing Snowflake-led wins with partner-submitted content while maintaining consistent quality and narrative flow.

The Deliverable: A downloadable eBook titled "Secrets of Snowflake Migration Success" that will be promoted across Snowflake.com, LinkedIn, email nurtures, sales follow-ups, and field events. It's designed to support pipeline acceleration, partner enablement, and campaigns tied to modernization initiatives.

KEY LEARNING
Not everything needs to be net-new to be valuable. By repurposing stories that were already approved and published, we avoided lengthy bottlenecks while still delivering fresh impact through strategic reframing. Pre-planning ebook assets and story pipelines in sync with sales programs (e.g., SQL Server now, Teradata next) means we can hit the ground running.
03. Key Learnings

What This Summer Taught Me

⏰
Plan for the Long Game
The MDF fund activation process takes time—longer than I initially expected. Building lead time (2+ quarters) into planning ensures funding, content, and promotion can all move in sync. This taught me the importance of strategic foresight in B2B marketing campaigns. For AI Data Cloud Day, MDF funds became available later in the cycle, meaning the initial live event leaned heavily on Snowflake-led channels. Next time, I'd start the partnership conversation even earlier.
🎯
Competitive Intelligence is Gold
Partnering with the Compete team was transformational for messaging development. Their insights helped sharpen positioning in ways that directly addressed competitor narratives. I learned that great messaging isn't just about what you say—it's about understanding the entire competitive landscape. The Compete team should be looped into more messaging exercises early. Their perspective makes the difference between generic positioning and sharp, defensible competitive angles.
🔄
Repurpose Smartly, Move Faster
Not everything needs to be net-new to be valuable. By repurposing already-approved customer stories and Summit content, I avoided lengthy approval bottlenecks while still delivering fresh impact through strategic reframing. This showed me that optimization and reuse of existing high-quality content can save time, resources, and still deliver fresh impact when framed in a new narrative. Speed matters in marketing—especially in fast-moving campaigns.
📞
Align Early, Align Often
Pre-planning content with sales alignment is critical. An asset like an eBook gains traction when it directly supports active sales motions. Starting earlier—building the next wave of stories (like Teradata) ahead of time—means hitting the ground running instead of ramping up after launch. For AI Data Cloud Day, activating sales enablement weeks before launch would have allowed AEs to use the value prop to open conversations and register accounts proactively.
🎪
Events Need End-to-End Thinking
Designing content so it's "born with multiple lives" is key. Content should flow naturally into marketing channels AND sales enablement from day one. For AI Data Cloud Day, we could have baked Summit cross-promotion into session design from the start so sessions naturally tied into follow-up campaigns. This creates momentum that lasts beyond the launch moment and maximizes the value of every content investment.
